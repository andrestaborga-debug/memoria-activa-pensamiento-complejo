Dialogic Tensions

Two opposing truths can both be necessary. The work is not to resolve the tension but to keep it alive and productive.

Morin's principe dialogique: complementary, concurrent, antagonistic. Not dialectic — no synthesis.

The idea

Much of our thinking about organizations is structured as a forced choice. Top-down or bottom-up. Accountability or ownership. Speed or depth. Freedom or alignment. Pick one. Build around it. Defend it.

Morin's dialogic principle is the observation that life and organization do not work this way. Living systems survive because they hold complementary forces in ongoing tension. Order and disorder, together, produce evolution — either alone produces stagnation or chaos. Stability and change, together, produce endurance — either alone is lethal. The forces are genuinely antagonistic, and genuinely necessary, simultaneously.

A team that has resolved its tensions has usually suppressed one pole. Its apparent harmony is the flatness of something half-alive. The most generative teams, by contrast, are the ones that have learned to articulate their tensions explicitly and oscillate between poles deliberately.

The tensions every team is holding

Speed and depth. Individual accountability and collective ownership. Freedom and alignment. Exploration and exploitation. Challenge and safety. Structure and emergence. Each of these is a dialogic pair. Trying to eliminate either pole produces predictable pathology: speed without depth produces technical debt; depth without speed produces irrelevance. The question is not which to pick. It is: how do we keep both alive, and how do we move between them with awareness?

In practice

Name the tension

Most dysfunctional meetings are unacknowledged fights between the two poles of an unnamed tension. Naming it dissolves the confusion without resolving the tension.

Oscillate deliberately

Some weeks, bias toward speed. Some weeks, bias toward depth. The error is freezing one pole as an identity.

Reward the loyal opposition

Someone on the team is probably carrying the under-weighted pole. They often feel like a dissenter. They are doing the system a favor.

Ban the forced choice

"Are we doing X or Y?" is often the wrong question. Try: "How are we holding both?"

A question to sit with

What tension am I trying to resolve that I should be cultivating?
